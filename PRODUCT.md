# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring teams, engineering managers, technical collaborators, and potential clients who are deciding whether Looped has the craft, reliability, and taste to build useful software with a strong creative edge. They arrive with limited attention, scan for proof of shipped work, then look for enough personality to remember the site.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio presents Looped as a full-stack developer and digital artist. It should turn project history into evidence: shipped products, technical range, performance awareness, and creative interface sensibility. Success means visitors can quickly understand what Looped builds, trust the work, and know how to start a conversation.

## Brand Personality

Expressive, technical, and artful. The site can feel more like a digital artist's authored space than a standard résumé, but credibility must stay visible. The voice should be specific, direct, and concrete, not buzzword-heavy or overly corporate.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS portfolio tropes, corporate templates, gamey neon overload, and minimal gray galleries that feel unfinished. Specifically avoid cream-gradient sameness, identical card grids, empty résumé polish, noisy cyberpunk effects, and monochrome layouts with too little authorship.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with authored craft: make the interface feel designed by the same person who builds the work.
2. Make proof easy to scan: project outcomes, technical choices, and links should be legible without decoding the layout.
3. Keep expression subordinate to trust: visual personality should improve memorability without hiding content or lowering readability.
4. Prefer concrete language: describe what was built, shipped, measured, or learned.
5. Design for repeat visits: the page should reward attention with detail, but still work as a fast portfolio scan.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use a strict conservative accessibility bar. Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard access, keep focus states visible, avoid content that depends on animation, and provide reduced-motion alternatives. Expressive effects are acceptable only when they preserve readability, do not create vestibular discomfort, and leave the default state usable.
